# Body and Ground

Choose a steady position. You can sit, stand, or lie down, and you can adjust at any point. Keep your eyes open if that helps you feel oriented. Begin by noticing the room: a color, a shape, and one sound that confirms where you are.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Now feel the ground or another surface beneath you. You do not have to imagine support. Look for direct evidence of it: pressure under the feet, the weight of the legs on a seat, the back meeting a chair, or the body resting on a bed.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Choose the clearest point of contact. Stay with its shape. Is it broad or small? Even or changing? Warm, cool, firm, or soft? If none of these descriptions fit, simply know that contact is present.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Let attention include a second point of support. Notice how the body's weight is distributed between the two. Nothing needs to be made symmetrical. The ground can receive you exactly as you are positioned now.

[Pause 20 seconds]

See whether you can release one task to the surface beneath you. Perhaps the feet do not need to press so hard. Perhaps the shoulders can stop holding themselves quite so high. Do not force a change. Ask, and notice the answer.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Feel breathing while you remain connected to the ground. On an inhale, the body may rise or widen a little. On an exhale, weight may settle. If no movement is clear, stay with pressure and contact instead.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Thoughts may pull attention upward and away. When that happens, return through something physical. Feel the heel, the seat, the back, or a hand resting on the body. Let one contact point be enough.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Now notice the edges of the body that are not touching a surface. Feel air at the face, around the arms, or across the hands. The body is both supported below and surrounded by space.

[Pause 20 seconds]

For a few moments, hold the whole pattern lightly: ground below, body here, air around, breath moving. You do not need to focus perfectly. Let awareness move among these simple facts and return whenever it wanders.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Check whether any area is asking for a small adjustment. Move slowly if you choose to move. Notice how support changes as the body changes position, and how the new position is received.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Let your gaze become clearer. Take in the room again and listen for the nearest sound, then the farthest sound you can hear. Keep one point of contact in awareness as you prepare to continue.

The ground does not remove what comes next. It gives you a place from which to meet it.
