# Counting the Breath

Find a position that feels stable enough for the next few minutes. Let the hands rest, soften your gaze, or close your eyes if that is comfortable. Notice two or three points where the body is supported. You can move, open your eyes, or stop whenever you choose.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin with the breath as it already is. There is no need to make it slower or deeper. Feel one complete inhale and one complete exhale. Choose the place where the movement is easiest to recognize.

[Pause 15 seconds]

We will use a light count to give attention a simple task. As the next exhale ends, silently say one. With the following exhale, say two. Continue until five, then begin again at one.

[Pause 30 seconds]

The count is not a test of memory. If you lose the number, discover that you have reached eight, or forget that you were counting, notice what happened without correcting the past. The next exhale can be one.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Continue from one to five. Let each number arrive near the end of the exhale. Leave the inhale uncounted and unhurried. One breath, one quiet number, then space for the next breath.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Check for unnecessary effort. Counting does not require a tight forehead, a held jaw, or a controlled breath. Let the face and hands receive a little more room. Keep only enough effort to recognize the exhale and remember the next number.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Begin another round. One. Two. Three. Four. Five. If the breathing rhythm changes, allow the counting rhythm to change with it. No breath has to wait for the number.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Now notice where attention tends to go. Perhaps into a plan, a sound, an image, or a judgment about the practice. The content does not need to be removed. Recognize thinking, feel one point of support, and let the next exhale be one.

[Pause 30 seconds]

For a while, count to ten instead. Use the same gentle approach. If ten creates strain or confusion, return to five. The useful part is not reaching the last number. It is recognizing the moment of return.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Release the count. Let two or three breaths pass without numbers. Notice whether the breath feels different when the small task is gone. There is no preferred result.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Bring the count back for one final round from one to five. See if the numbers can be quieter now, almost like markers placed beside the breath rather than instructions given to it.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Let the final number go. Feel the body as a whole, supported and breathing. Include sounds in the room without following them. Include thoughts without needing to finish them.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice the next movement before you make it. Let the eyes open or focus more clearly. Counting offered a path back, not a score. You can begin with one whenever you need a simple place to return.
