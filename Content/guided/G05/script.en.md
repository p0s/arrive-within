# Open Awareness

Settle in a position that feels steady and awake. Keep the eyes open if a wider view helps you stay present. Notice where you are, the time of day, and the surface supporting you. You remain free to move, narrow your attention, or end the practice.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin with one clear anchor. Feel the feet, the seat, the hands, or one ordinary breath. Let attention rest there without holding tightly. This anchor will remain available whenever the wider field feels busy or unclear.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Now include sounds. Let hearing receive whatever is already arriving: a close sound, a distant sound, a steady sound, or the space between sounds. You do not need to name the source. Notice that each sound appears, changes, and is no longer the same.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Keep hearing open and include sensation in the body. Pressure, temperature, movement, stillness, comfort, or discomfort may be present. Let sensations occupy their own places. There is no need to combine them into one story about how the body feels.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Return briefly to the anchor. One point of contact. One breath. Then allow the field to widen again.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Notice how attention chooses something from the field. A sound becomes prominent, then a sensation. A thought may move to the front. You do not have to prevent this movement. Recognize what is foreground now, and what remains quietly in the background.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Thoughts can be included as events too. You may notice words, pictures, memories, plans, or fragments that do not form a complete idea. Rather than entering the subject, notice the simple fact that thinking is happening. Then listen again. Feel the body again.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If a thought or feeling becomes too strong, make the field smaller. Open your eyes, name three visible objects, and feel both feet or another reliable support. Open awareness is a choice, not a demand to stay with something overwhelming.

[Pause 20 seconds]

When it feels workable, let sounds, sensations, and thoughts share the same space. Nothing has to be pushed away, and nothing has to receive all of your attention. Experience can come and go within a larger awareness.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice a moment of change. A sound ends. An itch moves. A breath turns. A thought loses its edge. You do not need to make change happen. The field is already changing on its own.

[Pause 35 seconds]

See whether there is also something steady: the weight of the body, the general presence of the room, or the simple capacity to notice. Let changing events and steady support be known together.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Release any effort to monitor everything. Open awareness does not mean tracking every detail. Let most experience remain unexamined. Receive what becomes clear, and allow it to pass back into the wider field.

[Pause 45 seconds]

For the next moments, notice only when something captures attention. Silently acknowledge hearing, feeling, or thinking. Then leave the label behind. There is no need to label the same event twice.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Now stop using labels. Rest as best you can with the whole field: body supported, air around you, sounds arriving, breath moving, thoughts appearing. If you become lost, return to the anchor and widen again when ready.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Begin to choose rather than simply receive. Select one sound and listen. Select one contact point and feel it. Select the next breath and accompany it. Notice that attention can be open and can also gather deliberately.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let the eyes focus clearly. Take in colors and edges. Feel the body in its full position and make any small movement that is needed. The wider field remains available as you continue, but you do not have to carry all of it. Meet the next thing while remembering there is space around it.
