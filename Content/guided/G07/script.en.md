# Sitting with Sound

Settle somewhere you can safely give attention to listening. Your eyes may stay open, softly focused, or closed. Feel the body supported and notice one point of contact that can help you remain oriented.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Begin with the nearest sound you can hear. It may come from your breathing, clothing, the room, or something just beyond it. Let the sound reach you. There is no need to lean toward it.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Now listen for a more distant sound. You do not need to identify its source. Notice its texture, rhythm, volume, or changing edge. If it disappears, let the disappearance be part of what you hear.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Allow near and far sounds into the same field. Some may be pleasant, some unwanted, and some neutral. Listening does not require you to approve of a sound. It only asks you to recognize that sound is present.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice how quickly the mind names what it hears: traffic, voice, machine, bird, music. Naming is natural. After the name appears, return to the direct qualities of the sound. High or low. Steady or broken. Close or distant.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Listen for the beginning of a sound. Then listen for its ending. Many sounds do not have a clear boundary, so notice whatever change is available. A sound may fade, be covered by another, or continue beyond your attention.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Include the quiet around sounds. Quiet does not have to mean complete silence. It can be the space in which small sounds become clear, or the brief interval before the next sound arrives.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If a sound pulls you into a story or reaction, feel the support beneath the body. Notice whether the reaction appears as tension, warmth, movement, or thought. Let that response belong to the field too, without needing to solve it.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now receive sound from every direction. In front, behind, to either side, above, or below. You do not need to map the room accurately. Let hearing be wide and effortless.

[Pause 40 seconds]

For a while, stop choosing sounds. Allow hearing to organize itself. A sound comes forward, changes, and gives way. Attention may follow. When you notice thinking, simply listen again.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Bring the sound of breathing into the wider field if it can be heard. If not, feel the breath while continuing to listen. Body, breath, and room are happening together.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Choose one sound for a final moment of close listening. Hear its detail without trying to hold it. Then release the choice and receive the whole soundscape again.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let your gaze become clear. Notice a visible shape and feel the body in its full position. Sounds will continue after the practice ends. You can use one of them as a simple invitation to arrive again.
