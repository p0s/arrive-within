# A Complete First Practice

Welcome. This practice will move through posture, body, breath, sound, and a few moments of open awareness. Nothing has to feel special. Choose a position you can change easily, and keep your eyes open whenever that feels more supportive. You can pause or stop at any time.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin by looking around. Notice three ordinary things in the room: perhaps a color, an edge, and a source of light. Hear one sound. Feel the surface beneath you. Let these simple facts confirm where you are.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Arrange the body with enough support. If you are sitting, let the feet or legs find a stable place. If you are lying down, allow the surface to receive your weight. Let the hands rest without needing a formal shape.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice the whole body at once. Not every detail, just the sense of a body taking up space. Feel where the body touches a surface and where it meets the air.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Move attention slowly through a few regions. Begin with the feet and lower legs. Notice pressure, temperature, movement, or very little sensation. All of these are valid experiences.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Include the hips and the places that carry weight. Then notice the hands and arms. There is no need to relax them. Let them be known as they are.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring attention to the shoulders, neck, and face. Notice the jaw and the space around the eyes. If a small adjustment is needed, make it slowly and feel the new position settle.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Now find the breath. Do not take a demonstration breath. Wait for the next inhale to arrive and notice where it can be felt most clearly: at the nose, in the chest, or around the belly.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Stay for one full inhale and one full exhale. Let the breath choose its own depth and timing.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Attention may move into a thought or sound. When you notice, feel one point of contact and return to the breath that is happening now. Wandering is not a detour from the practice; noticing it is part of the practice.

[Pause 35 seconds]

For a few breaths, silently use the words in and out if they help. Let the words remain quiet and simple. If they get in the way, release them.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Now let the breath move into the background and listen. Receive the closest sound you can hear. Then receive a more distant sound. You do not need to name either one.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice how sounds arrive without being invited. They change, overlap, and end. If a sound is unpleasant, you can keep some attention on the support beneath the body while acknowledging that the sound is present.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Widen listening in every direction. Let the room have its full soundscape. The breath continues, the body remains supported, and hearing opens around both.

[Pause 45 seconds]

We will now allow awareness to become a little wider. Include body sensations, breath, sound, and thoughts in one field. You do not need to keep track of everything. Notice only what becomes clear by itself.

[Pause 40 seconds]

A sensation may come forward. Then a sound. Then a thought or image. Each can be present for a moment and move back. If anything feels too strong, open your eyes, look around, and return to the feet or another steady contact.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Notice whether anything in experience stays completely still. A breath turns. A sound changes. Pressure shifts. A thought loses its place. You do not have to produce change; you can let change reveal itself.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Return to one anchor for a quieter minute. Choose the breath, the hands, the feet, or sound. Each time attention wanders, recognize the moment and begin again with the chosen anchor.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Release the anchor. Feel the whole body again, from the points of support to the top of the head. Include the room and the air around you.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice how you are meeting this final part. Perhaps calm is present, perhaps restlessness, tiredness, or no obvious change. The practice does not require a particular result. Showing up and noticing are enough.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Let the eyes focus clearly. Listen for one close and one distant sound. Move the fingers and toes, or make another small movement that feels right. Take your time changing position.

You have practiced several ways of returning: through the ground, the body, the breath, and sound. You do not need to remember all of them. Choose one simple doorway the next time you want to arrive.
