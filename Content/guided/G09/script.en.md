# Soften the Edges

Take a position that feels supported. Keep your eyes open if you prefer. Notice the general shape of this moment without trying to improve it: the body here, the room around you, and whatever mood or energy has arrived.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Find one place where effort is useful. Perhaps the back is holding you upright or the feet are keeping balance. Let that necessary effort remain.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Now look for effort that may not be needed. Check the space between the eyebrows, the tongue, the fingers, and the shoulders. Do not order anything to relax. Ask whether one area can use slightly less force.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Feel one breath pass through the body without changing it. On the exhale, imagine no special release. Simply notice whether the body makes any adjustment by itself.

[Pause 20 seconds]

If tension stays, let it stay. Soften the attention around it. Sense the nearby areas, the support beneath it, and the space around the body. One tight place does not have to become the whole field.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Check the jaw. The teeth can remain separated if that is comfortable. Let the lips find their own position. Then notice the hands. Feel each hand's weight rather than asking it to become loose.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice any effort in the mind: rehearsing, solving, or insisting that this practice work. See whether the next few seconds can remain unresolved. A thought may continue without being completed now.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Return to the useful effort that supports your posture. Keep what is needed. Allow what is not needed to become five percent quieter, if it wants to.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Feel the room again. Let your gaze move and make a small, comfortable adjustment. Softening is not collapse and it does not erase difficulty. It is a little more room around what is already here.
