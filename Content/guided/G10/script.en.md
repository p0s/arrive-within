# A Pause Under Pressure

Before you begin, make sure you are somewhere you can safely give part of your attention to this practice. Keep your eyes open if you need to stay oriented.

Pressure can make everything seem equally urgent. For these minutes, you do not need to solve the whole situation. Begin by noticing one fact: you are here, in this place, in this body.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Feel the support beneath you. Let your feet meet the floor, or notice the weight of your body on the chair or bed. Name three things you can see without searching for anything special.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Now hear two sounds. One may be near and one farther away. Let sound remind you that this moment is larger than the demand occupying your mind.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Notice one place in the body that is working hard. Perhaps the jaw, the shoulders, the hands, or the belly. You do not have to make it relax. Ask only whether five percent less effort is available.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Let the breath remain natural. On an inhale, silently say, here. On an exhale, silently say, now. If those words do not help, simply feel one full breath from beginning to end.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Thoughts about what must happen next may return. You can acknowledge them: planning is here, worry is here, urgency is here. Then return to the contact of your body with support.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Choose one demand that can wait for the length of three breaths. Not forever. Just three breaths.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Now ask a practical question: what is the next workable action, not every action? It may be sending one message, writing one line, asking for help, drinking water, or taking a longer pause. You do not need the perfect answer.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel both feet or another steady point. Notice the room again. Let your gaze move. Take in one more ordinary breath.

The pressure may still be present. This pause does not promise to remove it. It gives you a little more space from which to meet what comes next.
