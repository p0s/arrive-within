# Steady in Uncertainty

Choose a place where you can pause safely. Sit, lie down, or stand with reliable support. Your eyes may stay open. This practice will not predict what happens next or ask you to feel certain. Its purpose is smaller: to stay near what is known in this moment while an unfinished question remains unfinished.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin by noticing the room. Find the nearest wall or edge, the direction of the door, and one source of light. Let the eyes move slowly enough to register that you are here, now, in a particular place.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Feel the points where the body meets support. Notice weight, pressure, temperature, and texture. You do not need to relax. Let contact be factual: the floor is here; the chair is here; the surface is carrying this much of you.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Uncertainty often pulls attention into a future made of many possible scenes. Notice if the mind is rehearsing, forecasting, or searching. There is no need to stop it by force. Silently name what is happening: planning, imagining, checking, waiting.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Now identify three facts that are true in the present and do not require interpretation. Perhaps: I am sitting. I can hear a sound. My hands are touching. Choose your own plain facts.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let one natural breath pass while you stay with the first fact. Another breath with the second. Another with the third. The breath is only a measure of time; it does not have to become slow or deep.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring the unfinished question to mind only as words, not as a full scene. Keep it at a workable distance. You might say: I do not know yet. Notice what happens in the body when those words are present.

[Pause 25 seconds]

If the response feels too strong, open your attention immediately to the room, look toward the door or window, and press gently into the surface beneath you. You decide how much contact to have with the question.

[Pause 20 seconds]

If it remains workable, locate one edge of the uncertainty in the body. Perhaps a moving stomach, a tight throat, energy in the arms, or pressure behind the eyes. Then locate one neutral or steady place. Move between them without needing either one to change.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Uncertainty is not the same as danger, though the body may react as if an answer is urgently needed. Check the present environment. Is there an action required right now for safety or care? If yes, the practice can end and you can take it. If no, allow this minute to be a minute of waiting.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice the difference between what can be done and what cannot be completed yet. A message can be drafted. A question can be written down. Information can be gathered later. Another person’s decision, tomorrow’s result, or the whole future may not be available now.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Imagine placing the unavailable part on a nearby shelf, not throwing it away. It remains important and can be picked up again. For these few breaths, let your hands be empty.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Feel the palms and fingers. Notice whether they are gripping, pressing, or preparing. Let them rest against a surface. Keep the effort that protects or supports you; release only the effort that is working on a future answer right now.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Return to the body’s outline. Feel the front, back, sides, and the space around you. Uncertainty may be one experience inside this wider shape. It is not the whole of you.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Offer yourself a sentence that is believable. I can know more later. I can take the next clear step. I do not have to finish this question in this breath. If none fits, use simply: not known yet.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Let silence follow the sentence. Notice any impulse to repeat it quickly or turn it into reassurance. The words do not promise an outcome. They mark the boundary between this moment and what has not arrived.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Consider one value you want to carry while you wait: honesty, patience, steadiness, kindness, courage, or care. A value is not a prediction. It is a way of meeting the next available action.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Ask what the smallest value-aligned step might be. It may be doing nothing for now. It may be writing one note, asking one clear question, resting, eating, or returning to the task in front of you. Keep the step small enough to be real.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Come back to three present facts. A sound. A point of contact. A color or shape. Notice whether they are the same facts as before or different ones. The present is allowed to keep changing without providing the final answer.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Take three ordinary breaths. With each exhale, feel the support beneath you. With each inhale, notice the room around you. No special rhythm is required.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Begin to move the head, hands, and feet. Look toward where you will go next. Let the unfinished question remain on its shelf until you deliberately choose to return to it.

Continue with the next clear thing, not the entire unknown future.
