# Deep Rest Without Sleep

Choose a place where resting is safe. You may lie down, recline, or sit with the back, head, and feet supported. Adjust anything that would make the body work unnecessarily. Keep your eyes open if that feels steadier. This is not a practice for staying alert during an activity. There is nothing to achieve, and sleep is not the goal. If sleep comes, that is not a mistake. If you remain awake, nothing has gone wrong.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Notice the room before turning inward. Take in the direction of light, the nearest sound, and the surface beneath you. Let the room remain part of the practice. You can open your eyes, move, or stop whenever you choose.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Feel the broad outline of the body. The places touching support. The places surrounded by air or fabric. You do not need to scan every detail. Let the whole shape be here at once.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice where the surface receives your weight most clearly. Perhaps the back of the head, shoulders, back, pelvis, legs, or feet. There is no need to press down. Let gravity and the surface meet each other without your help.

[Pause 30 seconds]

The body may still be holding itself in small ways. That is allowed. Rest does not require perfect release. Simply notice one place that is working: the forehead, tongue, fingers, belly, or toes. Ask whether five percent less effort is available. If not, let it be exactly as it is.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring attention to the eyes. They may be closed, softly focused, or looking at one steady point. Feel the small muscles around them without demanding softness. Let seeing or not seeing take very little work.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice the jaw and mouth. The teeth can remain apart if that is comfortable. The tongue can rest wherever it naturally lands. Swallow or adjust when you need to. Nothing has to become loose.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Feel the shoulders and arms. Sense their weight traveling through elbows, forearms, wrists, and hands. Imagine that the surface is willing to hold the full weight of each arm. You do not have to make the arms heavy; just notice how much is already being carried.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Let the hands be unoccupied. Fingers may curl, open, touch one another, or rest on clothing. Notice temperature in the palms and the quiet shape of the hands. For these moments, they do not need to hold, reach, type, or prepare.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring attention to the back of the body. Feel the areas of clear contact and the small spaces where the body curves away from support. Let both contact and space belong. If discomfort asks for a change, move slowly and arrange the support again.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice the front of the body breathing. There may be movement in the chest, ribs, belly, or somewhere less obvious. Do not deepen the breath. Let each inhale arrive in its own size, and let each exhale finish without being pushed.

[Pause 35 seconds]

For three breaths, notice only the moment when effort changes. The inhale may gather a little energy. The exhale may release some, or may simply be an exhale. No response is required.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel the pelvis and legs. Notice their broad weight and the way it is distributed. One side may feel different from the other. Let the thighs, knees, lower legs, and feet be supported without needing symmetry.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Bring attention to the feet. Feel heels, soles, toes, socks, bedding, floor, or air. The feet have no next place to reach during this practice. They can remain here.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now sense the body from head to feet as one resting shape. Some areas may feel settled. Others may be restless, alert, numb, uncomfortable, or unclear. Deep rest can include all of that. You are not waiting for a special state before rest is allowed.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Thoughts may continue. Plans, fragments, images, and unfinished conversations may appear. You do not need to empty the mind. When you notice a thought, let it be one small event inside a larger field that also contains weight, sound, temperature, and breath.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Listen to the nearest sound, then a sound farther away. Let hearing happen without following the story of the sound. Notice the quiet that surrounds or follows it.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return to the feeling of being held. The surface does not ask whether you are resting well. It receives the weight that is here. Let the next exhale end, and wait without hurry for the next inhale.

[Pause 45 seconds]

If wakefulness is strong, it does not need to be defeated. Feel wakefulness as energy in the body: brightness behind the eyes, movement in the chest, active thoughts, or an urge to shift. Give that energy room while the body remains supported.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If drowsiness is present, you do not need to hold on to the instructions. Sounds may move to the background. If you lose the thread and return later, begin again with one point of contact. Nothing was missed.

[Pause 45 seconds]

For a little while, release the task of following. Let sound, breath, and sensation come and go without arranging them.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Begin to notice the room again. Light, temperature, and sounds. Feel the outline of the body and the places where it meets support. There is no need to decide whether the practice worked.

[Pause 25 seconds]

If you are continuing to rest, you may let the guidance end here. If you are getting up, invite movement into fingers and toes. Bend or stretch only as your body prefers. Roll or sit slowly enough to remain oriented, pausing before you stand.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Rest did not require sleep, stillness, or a blank mind. For these minutes, the body was allowed to be supported without having to produce a result.
