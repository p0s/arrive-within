# Short Body Scan

Settle in a position that is safe and reasonably supported. The eyes may close, soften, or stay open. There is no need to become still. Small adjustments are welcome.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Begin with the places where the body meets a surface. Notice pressure, temperature, firmness, or perhaps very little. You do not need to name the right sensation. Simply let contact be known.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Bring attention to the feet. Feel both feet together, or one at a time. Notice toes, soles, heels, and the space around them. If the feet are difficult to sense, feel the socks, shoes, floor, or air instead.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Let attention include the lower legs, knees, and upper legs. Sensation may be clear, faint, pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. Nothing has to change. Notice the weight of the legs and how they are being supported.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Move toward the pelvis, abdomen, and lower back. The breath may create movement here, or it may not be obvious. Let the breath remain natural. Feel one whole region rather than searching for a special experience.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Include the chest and upper back. Notice clothing, warmth, movement, or stillness. If this area feels uncomfortable, return to a steady point of contact such as the feet or seat. You choose where attention rests.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Feel both hands. Notice fingers, palms, and the backs of the hands. Let the arms join the picture, from wrists through elbows to shoulders. The shoulders do not need to drop. They can simply be felt as they are.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Bring attention to the neck, jaw, and face. Notice the mouth, eyes, forehead, and scalp without asking them to relax. Allow expressions and sensations to be present without correction.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Now sense the body as a whole: contact below, space around, breath moving somewhere within. Some areas may stand out and others may remain quiet. Both belong.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice one sound in the room. Let the eyes take in light and shape. Make the next movement at a comfortable pace, carrying this broader sense of the body with you.
