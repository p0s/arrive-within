# Full Body Scan

Find a position where the body can remain supported for a while. Lying down may be comfortable, or you can sit with the back and feet well supported. Keep your eyes open if that helps you feel oriented. Nothing in this practice requires stillness; change position whenever you need to.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Begin by noticing the whole shape of the body at once. Not every detail. Just the sense of a body here, taking up space, touching a surface, surrounded by air.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel the places where weight is received. The back of the head, the shoulders, the back, the pelvis, the legs, or the feet. Let the supporting surface do its share of the work.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Bring attention to the toes of the left foot. There may be warmth, coolness, pressure, tingling, pulsing, or no clear sensation. No sensation is also information. Include the sole, the heel, and the top of the foot.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Notice the left ankle and lower leg. Feel the skin, the deeper weight, or the shape you know is there even when sensation is quiet. Move through the calf and shin toward the knee.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Let the left knee be as it is. Notice the front, sides, and back of the joint. Then include the thigh, its contact with clothing or support, and the place where the leg meets the pelvis.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Allow the whole left leg to be present at once, from hip to toes. You do not need to hold it in attention tightly. Let it rest in a wider field.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Move to the toes of the right foot. Sense whatever is easiest to notice. The spaces between toes. The sole. The heel. The top of the foot. There is no need for the two sides to feel the same.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Notice the right ankle, calf, and shin. Perhaps there are places that feel vivid and places that seem blank. Let both belong.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Bring attention through the right knee and thigh. Notice pressure, temperature, fabric, or weight. Include the whole right leg from hip to toes.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Feel both legs together. Their weight. Their length. Any difference between them. Let the next breath move while the legs remain supported.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Bring attention to the pelvis and hips. Notice points of contact and the broad weight of this central part of the body. Sense the lower belly from the inside, without asking it to soften or change.

[Pause 18 seconds]

The breath may create movement in the belly. Feel expansion, release, or very little movement. Let each breath have its own size.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Move into the lower back. Perhaps you feel the support beneath it, the curve above it, or only a general area. Let attention include the sides of the waist and the whole lower torso.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Notice the middle and upper back. Feel broad contact, individual points, warmth, coolness, ease, or effort. There is nothing to correct. If discomfort is asking for movement, adjust slowly and let the scan continue from the new position.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Bring attention to the chest and ribs. Notice breathing from the front, sides, or back. The heart may be felt as a pulse, or it may not be noticeable. Do not search for it. Let the whole torso breathe.

[Pause 22 seconds]

Now move to the fingers of the left hand. Feel fingertips, nails, palms, and the back of the hand. Notice whether the hand is open, curled, warm, cool, heavy, or light.

[Pause 16 seconds]

Include the left wrist and forearm. Travel through the elbow and upper arm to the shoulder. Sense the whole arm resting in its position.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Bring attention to the fingers of the right hand. Notice contact between fingers or with fabric. Feel the palm and back of the hand, then the wrist and forearm.

[Pause 16 seconds]

Move through the right elbow and upper arm into the shoulder. Let both arms be present together, from shoulders to fingertips.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice the shoulders. They do not have to drop. Simply feel their shape and weight. Include the places where the arms join the torso and the upper chest meets the neck.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Bring attention to the throat and neck. Sense the front of the throat, the sides, and the back of the neck. Swallow if you need to. Let the jaw find a position that does not require extra holding.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Feel the chin, lips, cheeks, and nose. Notice air at the nostrils if it is clear. Include the eyes resting in their sockets, the forehead, the temples, and the scalp.

[Pause 22 seconds]

Notice the whole head. Sounds may be part of this moment too. Let hearing remain open while you sense the weight and temperature of the head.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Now widen attention to include the entire body. Head, arms, torso, pelvis, legs, and feet. Some areas may be distinct and others vague. The body does not need to become one uniform feeling.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the body breathing as a whole. Breath entering. Breath leaving. Count three natural exhalations if that helps you remain here, then let go of the count.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice that sensations change without asking permission. A pulse appears. Warmth spreads. An itch comes and goes. Stillness contains many small movements.

[Pause 25 seconds]

If attention has wandered, receive the return without criticism. Come back to the broad contact with support. The whole body, here.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Begin to recognize the room around the body. The air on exposed skin. Sounds near and far. Light through the eyelids or shapes in front of you.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Invite a little movement into the fingers and toes. Rotate the wrists or ankles if that feels good. Stretch only in a way that suits your body today.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Before changing position, notice one final time how the surface holds you. Then let the eyes open or refocus, and move at your own pace.

The scan is complete. There was nothing particular you had to feel, and no state you had to achieve.
