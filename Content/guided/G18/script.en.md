# Shoulders, Jaw, Hands

Settle somewhere safe and still, with the feet, seat, or back supported. This practice includes optional small movements. Make them smaller, imagine them, or leave them out if movement is uncomfortable. Nothing should be forced through pain.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Begin by noticing the shoulders without changing them. Sense their height, weight, temperature, and contact with clothing. One may sit higher or feel clearer than the other. Let the difference be information rather than a problem.

[Pause 18 seconds]

If it feels comfortable, lift both shoulders a very small amount. Notice the effort of lifting. Then stop lifting and let gravity choose where they land. This is not a command to relax; it is a chance to feel the contrast between doing and not doing.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Try the movement once more, perhaps one shoulder at a time. Lift slightly. Pause. Release the action. Notice whether any effort continues after the movement ends.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Bring attention to the jaw. Feel where the upper and lower teeth meet, or the space between them. Notice the tongue, lips, cheeks, and hinges near the ears. You do not need to open the mouth or change the bite.

[Pause 18 seconds]

If it is comfortable, let the lips part a little and move the jaw gently from side to side. Keep the range easy. Then let the jaw settle in a position that asks for no extra holding.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice whether the tongue is pressing, hovering, or resting. Let it choose a broad, ordinary place in the mouth. Swallow if you need to. Feel the jaw again without checking whether it is relaxed enough.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Now feel both hands. Notice the backs, palms, fingers, and spaces between fingers. Are the hands holding one another, clothing, a device, or nothing? If you are holding something safely, sense the exact amount of pressure being used.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Slowly curl the fingers toward the palms without making a tight fist. Feel the work of closing. Then uncurl them and let the fingers find their own shape.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Touch each fingertip to the thumb, one at a time, or imagine the sequence. Notice texture, temperature, and the precise beginning and ending of each contact.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Let the hands rest again. Trace a line of sensation from fingers through wrists and forearms to the shoulders. The hands, jaw, and shoulders may influence one another, or they may not. There is no result to manufacture.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Take one ordinary breath. As you exhale, stop any movement you are still making. Notice what the body does when the instructions become quiet.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Include the whole body around these three areas: feet or seat supported, breath moving, room nearby. Shoulders, jaw, and hands do not need to remain soft. You have simply met them with attention and a little more choice.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Let the eyes take in the room. Make the next movement slowly enough to notice it, and continue with whatever comes next.
