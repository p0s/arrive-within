# Warmth and Weight

Choose a safe resting position, lying down or well supported. Adjust blankets, clothing, or posture if you are physically too warm, cold, numb, or uncomfortable. This practice is about noticing ordinary sensation, not enduring discomfort or creating a particular temperature.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin with the places where the body meets support. Feel the broad fact of contact before searching for detail. The surface may feel firm, soft, cool, warm, or difficult to describe.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice where weight is clearest. Perhaps the head, shoulders, back, pelvis, legs, or heels. Let one region be heavy enough to be received by the surface. You do not need to make it heavier.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring attention to the hands. One hand may feel warmer than the other. The palms may hold warmth while the backs feel cool, or there may be no clear difference. Stay with what is actually present.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice the temperature of air or fabric at the fingers. Then notice the deeper sense of the hands' weight. Temperature and weight can be known together without needing to choose between them.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Move attention to the feet. Feel socks, bedding, floor, or air. Perhaps there is warmth, coolness, tingling, pressure, or very little. If sensation is unclear, notice the known shape and position of the feet instead.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let both legs enter awareness. Sense their length and broad weight. One leg may seem heavier, lighter, warmer, or cooler. The two sides do not need to match.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice where the legs are supported and where there is space beneath them. Let the support carry what it can. If the body asks for a small adjustment, make it and feel how the map of pressure changes.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring attention to the pelvis and lower back. This central region may feel dense, light, broad, warm, cool, comfortable, or uncomfortable. Use simple words if words help, then return to direct sensation.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the belly and ribs moving with an ordinary breath. The inhale may bring a slight lift or expansion. The exhale may bring a sense of settling, or no change at all. Do not push the body toward heaviness.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice the chest and upper back. Temperature may vary where clothing, air, or support touches the skin. Weight may be clearer at the back than the front. Let the whole torso be one changing field.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring attention to the shoulders and arms. Feel the weight of the upper arms, elbows, forearms, and hands as one continuous path. Imagine no change; simply recognize what the surface is already holding.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice the face. Air may feel cool near the nose and warmth may gather around the eyes or cheeks. There may be no contrast. Include the weight of the head and the place where it is supported.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now widen attention from head to feet. Warm areas, cool areas, heavy areas, light or almost absent areas. None is more correct. Sensation can shift while you listen.

[Pause 40 seconds]

See whether warmth has an edge. Does it stop clearly or fade into another temperature? See whether weight has an edge, or spreads into the support beneath you. There is no need to answer precisely.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice a sound in the room while continuing to feel the body. Sound has no weight, yet it can share the same moment with pressure, temperature, and breath.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Let attention rest on the single clearest sensation, whether warm, cool, heavy, light, or neutral. Stay for three natural breaths. If the sensation changes, follow the change without trying to keep it.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Return to the whole body's contact with support. Gravity continues without instruction. The surface continues to respond. For a few moments, let being carried be enough.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Begin to recognize the room more fully. Notice air on the skin, light through the eyes or eyelids, and sounds near and far. Feel the body as both weighted and able to move.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Invite a small movement into fingers and toes. If you are getting up, change position slowly and pause before standing. If you are continuing to rest, let the movement fade.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Warmth, coolness, weight, and lightness did not need to be produced. You practiced noticing how the body is here, and how support meets it now.
