# Walking Meditation

Choose a safe, clear, obstacle-free place where walking slowly will not put you or anyone else at risk. Do not use this practice near traffic, stairs, water, or anywhere that requires your full external attention. You may stop, open your eyes more fully, or end the practice whenever needed.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Stand in a way that feels steady. Let your eyes remain open with a soft, useful gaze. Notice the contact of both feet with the ground and the ordinary work of balancing.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Before moving, feel one breath arrive and leave. There is no need to coordinate breathing with steps. Let the breath keep its own rhythm while the body prepares to walk.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Begin at a comfortable pace. It can be slower than usual, but it does not need to look unusual. Feel the first shift of weight from one foot toward the other.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice one foot becoming lighter. The heel lifts, then the rest of the foot follows. The leg moves through space, and the foot meets the ground again. You do not need to name every part while it happens.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Let the other step unfold. Weight transfers, one foot releases, travels, and returns. Walking already knows how to organize itself. Your task is only to accompany a few sensations.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Keep enough attention on the path ahead to remain safe. Within that wider awareness, notice pressure changing across the soles: heel, middle, toes, or a pattern that does not fit those words.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel how the knees and hips participate. The pelvis shifts, the arms may swing, and the spine responds. Walking is a whole-body movement, even when attention begins at the feet.

[Pause 30 seconds]

If you reach the end of your path, pause before turning. Look where you are going. Turn in several ordinary steps, regain a steady direction, and continue only when the way is clear.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice sounds and changes in light without chasing them. Let the walking body remain part of a real place. Awareness can include the environment and sensation together.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Perhaps the mind has moved into plans, memories, or commentary. When you notice, use the next clear footfall as a place to return. No criticism is needed; returning is the practice.

[Pause 30 seconds]

For several steps, feel the moment just before the foot lands. Then feel contact spread as weight is received. Let the ground be firm enough to meet each step.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Now include the space between steps: the brief moment when weight is moving and balance is alive. There is no need to hold the body still inside movement.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Allow your pace to become a little more natural. See whether attention can stay present without making the walk stiff or mechanical. Fluid movement and careful awareness can belong together.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice effort. Some muscles work and others release. If the body is strained, shorten the path, slow down, or stop. Comfort is not required, but safety and workable effort matter.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Choose three steps to know clearly from beginning to end. One step. Then another. Then one more. Afterward, let counting go and continue with a broad, uncomplicated awareness.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Begin to finish by returning to your starting place or another safe stopping point. Let the pace settle. Stand still only after both feet have found reliable contact.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Feel the after-sensation of walking: warmth, pulsing, steadiness, movement still echoing, or nothing distinct. Notice one breath while standing.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Look around fully before you move on. This practice did not require leaving ordinary life. You practiced arriving inside an ordinary action, one safe step at a time.
