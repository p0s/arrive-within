# Breath in the Belly

Find a seated or resting position that gives the belly room to move. Loosen anything that feels restrictive if that is comfortable and appropriate. This is a practice of noticing breathing, not changing how much air you take in.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Let the eyes close or keep a soft gaze. Feel the support beneath the body. Notice that breathing is already happening before you direct attention toward it.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Bring one hand to the belly if touch feels helpful. Otherwise, let both hands rest elsewhere. There is no correct place to feel the breath and no need for visible movement.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice what happens around the belly as an inhale arrives. There may be a small outward movement, a widening at the sides, a change deeper inside, or very little you can detect.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice the exhale. The belly may soften inward or downward. It may barely move. Let each breath finish without pushing out the last air.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Stay with three ordinary breaths. Receive the inhale as it is. Follow the exhale as it is. If breathing feels tight, uneven, shallow, or full, let that be information rather than a problem to solve.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Perhaps the hand can feel movement that attention alone did not notice. Perhaps touch is distracting. You can move the hand away at any point. Choice is part of staying present.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Widen awareness to include the lower ribs and the back of the waist. Breathing may create a three-dimensional change around the middle of the body, even when one area is clearer than another.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Let the next inhale begin by itself. Notice its beginning, its continuing, and the moment it turns. Then notice the exhale beginning without your command.

[Pause 30 seconds]

There may be a brief pause after breathing out, or the next inhale may arrive immediately. Do not manufacture a pause. Let the body's timing be enough.

[Pause 30 seconds]

If the mind starts counting, evaluating, or trying to improve the breath, recognize the effort gently. Return to one simple sensation: movement, pressure, warmth, or contact beneath a hand.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice whether the belly feels different at the beginning and end of an exhale. The difference may be clear, subtle, or absent. Precision is not required.

[Pause 30 seconds]

For a few breaths, include the whole body. The belly moves within a body that is supported, hearing sounds, sensing temperature, and occupying a room. Attention can be steady without becoming narrow.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return once more to the easiest place to feel breathing around the belly. Let five breaths pass without adjusting their depth or rhythm. If discomfort, dizziness, or air hunger appears, stop focusing on breath and orient to the room.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Release any hand from the belly. Notice the trace of warmth or pressure left by contact. Feel the body's weight and the ordinary breath continuing on its own.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Open the gaze more fully. You did not need to breathe perfectly. You practiced meeting the movement that was already here, one unforced breath at a time.
