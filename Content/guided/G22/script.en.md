# A Clear Start

Before beginning the next task, pause in a safe position. Let your hands rest for a moment. Notice the surface beneath you and one sound nearby.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Something came before this pause. It may feel finished, unfinished, satisfying, or difficult. You do not need to review it now. On the next exhale, imagine setting it beside you rather than carrying it into every part of what comes next.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Feel one ordinary breath arrive and leave. Do not make it deeper for the sake of focus. Let the breath show that this moment has already begun.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Notice where attention is being pulled. There may be several tabs, messages, worries, or ideas asking to go first. Name them quietly as many things, without deciding that any of them is wrong.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Now choose one next point of attention. Not the whole project. Not the perfect outcome. Choose the first visible action: open the document, read the question, place the materials, write one sentence, or make one call.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Say that action silently in simple words. If it is still too large, make it smaller until it can be started without a debate.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Feel the body that will do this action. Notice feet or seat, hands, and the direction of the gaze. Let the posture become alert enough, not rigid. Keep whatever support makes the task sustainable.

[Pause 18 seconds]

For three natural breaths, let the chosen action be the only item you rehearse. When another thought appears, acknowledge it and return to this one beginning.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Check whether anything essential is missing: water, a tool, a boundary, or a brief message that prevents confusion. Add only what is genuinely needed to start.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Look at the place where the first action will happen. This practice does not guarantee concentration, and wandering later is not failure. It gives you a clear return point.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Take one final breath. As it leaves, move directly into the small action you named. Begin there.
