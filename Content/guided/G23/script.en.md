# Single-Point Focus

Choose a seated or resting posture that can remain workable for a while. Let the body be alert enough to notice and supported enough that discomfort does not have to become the center of the practice.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Select one simple sensory point. It might be breath at the nostrils, contact in one hand, pressure beneath the feet, or one steady sound. Choose something neutral or mildly pleasant rather than intense.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Let this point become the home for attention. You are not trying to exclude the rest of experience. You are giving the mind one clear place to return.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice the detail that is available now. At the nostrils, perhaps coolness, warmth, or movement. In a hand, perhaps pressure, tingling, temperature, or simple contact.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Stay for one complete cycle of the sensation. If the point changes with breathing, follow one inhale and one exhale. If it is steadier, remain for the length of one natural breath.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Attention will move. A sound, thought, feeling, or body sensation may become more compelling. The moment you know attention has moved is already a moment of awareness.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Name the movement quietly if that helps: thinking, hearing, planning, remembering. Then let the label go and return to the chosen point without arguing with what appeared.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the next clear contact with the point. Returning does not need to be sharp or forceful. It can be like setting down a light object with care.

[Pause 35 seconds]

For several breaths, practice this simple sequence: know the point, notice wandering, return. Success is not uninterrupted concentration. Success is an honest return.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Perhaps attention stays for only a second. Begin again. Perhaps it stays longer. There is no need to hold it tightly or compare this moment with the previous one.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice whether effort has gathered in the forehead, eyes, jaw, shoulders, or hands. Let unnecessary effort soften while keeping the chosen point clear.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now become curious about the beginning of each contact. Where does the next inhale first become knowable? When does pressure first register? Let freshness support attention.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Then notice the ending. A sensation fades, changes, or gives way to another. The point is stable enough to return to and alive enough never to be exactly the same.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If the chosen point becomes uncomfortable or emotionally charged, select another neutral anchor. Changing wisely is not failure. The practice is steady attention, not endurance.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Return again. Let sounds remain in the background. Let thoughts pass through the wider field. Keep the point in the foreground without needing to push anything away.

[Pause 45 seconds]

For the next five breaths, count only the returns. Each time attention comes home, recognize that movement with quiet appreciation. Begin at one again whenever needed.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Release counting. Stay with the point for one final stretch, relaxed and interested. Notice not only the sensation but also the quality of attending to it.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Let the focus widen to include the whole body and the room. The chosen point remains part of a larger field. Hear sounds, feel support, and notice light through open or closed eyes.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Move fingers or shoulders if that feels useful. You practiced returning, not perfection. That same return can be available during the next ordinary task.
