# Before Deep Work

Settle somewhere safe to pause before you begin. Let the work wait outside these few minutes. You are not falling behind by preparing your attention.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Feel the posture you have brought here. If it helps, place both feet down and let the spine rise without becoming rigid. Allow the shoulders to hang from that support.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Notice the residue of what came before: a conversation, a message, another task, or a thought still moving. You do not need to erase it. Silently say, that was before. This is the transition.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Bring attention to the physical sensation of breathing. Choose one place where the breath is clear enough. Stay for three cycles, counting each exhale from one to three.

[Pause 20 seconds]

When another concern appears, write it down after the practice if it truly needs remembering. For now, let it sit at the edge of awareness. Return to the next exhale.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Widen attention. Hear the room. Feel the whole body sitting here. Then narrow attention again to a single point of breath. Wide. And narrow.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Focused work does not require shutting out the world by force. It asks you to recognize what belongs to this period and what can remain outside it.

Name the one piece of work you are about to meet. Keep it small and concrete. Not finish everything. Perhaps read the first page, shape the first paragraph, examine the next result, or make the first decision.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Choose a simple way to return when you drift: feel one breath, look again at the question, or place a hand on the desk. Drifting will happen. Returning is the practice.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Now feel your hands. Notice the light and the space in front of you. Let the eyes focus more clearly.

Carry one intention into the work: steady, curious, and willing to begin with what is here.
