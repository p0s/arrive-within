# Kindness Toward Yourself

Choose a posture that feels sufficiently supported. This practice does not ask you to produce warmth, forgiveness, or any special emotion. It offers a way to speak to yourself without adding unnecessary harm.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Notice the points where the body is held by a chair, bed, floor, or cushion. Let support be a fact before it becomes a feeling.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring to mind that you are a person having this particular moment. You do not need to summarize your life or decide whether the moment is good or bad.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice one area of strain, tiredness, or ordinary difficulty. Choose something manageable rather than the most painful experience available. You can return to neutral sensation at any time.

[Pause 30 seconds]

See whether you can name the difficulty simply: this is tiring, this hurts, this is uncertain, or this is a lot. Use words that feel true rather than dramatic.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now try a steady response: may I meet this moment without turning against myself. If that wording does not fit, use: I can be on my own side for this breath.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Listen to the phrase as sound and meaning. You do not have to believe it completely. Let it be an offered direction rather than a test.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Place a hand where contact feels grounding, perhaps on the chest, arm, or thigh. Or keep both hands where they are. Touch is optional and does not make the practice more real.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel one breath beneath or near that contact. On the exhale, release only what releases naturally. Kindness does not require the body to relax on command.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Offer another believable phrase: may I give myself the same basic consideration I would give someone who is trying. Adjust the words until they sound honest.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Perhaps resistance appears: I do not deserve this, these words are false, or this is uncomfortable. Notice resistance without making it an enemy. You might say: resistance is allowed to be here too.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return to the supporting surface. Feel how it holds weight without asking for an explanation. Let physical support accompany the words.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Recall one recent effort, however small: getting through a difficult hour, answering a message, resting, beginning again, or showing up now. Acknowledge the effort without inflating it.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Try: this effort counts. Or: I can recognize what I carried. Notice whether either phrase feels useful, neutral, or not right today.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If no phrase works, let kindness take the form of less pressure. For several breaths, stop demanding a result from this practice.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice the tone of your inner voice now. You are not required to make it sweet. Aim for clear, respectful, and no harsher than necessary.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Offer one final sentence for the next part of the day: may I take the next workable step. Let the step remain modest and specific.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Widen attention to the whole body and the room. Release any hand placement. Hear sounds and notice light while keeping a thread of respectful attention toward yourself.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Move when you are ready. Kindness here was not a mood you had to achieve. It was the practice of meeting difficulty without becoming another source of it.
