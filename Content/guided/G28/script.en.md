# Making Room for Feelings

Choose a supported seated or resting position. This practice is for a manageable feeling, not an emergency or an experience that overwhelms your ability to stay oriented. If intensity rises, open your eyes, move, contact support, or end the practice.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin with the room. Name silently three neutral things you can see, then notice one sound and one place where the body meets support.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Feel both feet, the back of the body, or another reliable contact point. Let this be your anchor. You can stay here without approaching any emotion.

[Pause 25 seconds]

If it feels workable, acknowledge a feeling that is present at a mild or moderate level. You do not need its complete story. A broad word such as sadness, irritation, worry, tenderness, or uncertainty is enough.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice what happens when you name it. The word may fit, partly fit, or feel wrong. You can replace it with a simpler phrase: something difficult is here.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Return to the anchor for one full breath. Feel support more strongly than you follow the feeling. There is room for both.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now approach one small physical expression of the emotion. Perhaps pressure in the chest, energy in the hands, warmth in the face, heaviness, hollowness, or restlessness.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Choose an area no larger than a hand. Notice its edges, intensity, temperature, and movement for only a few seconds. Then return to a neutral contact point.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Move back and forth at your own pace: a small piece of feeling, then support. Feeling, then the room. You decide how close and for how long.

[Pause 45 seconds]

If thoughts tell a story about why the feeling exists, acknowledge thinking and return to present sensation. The story may matter later; it does not have to be solved now.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice whether the feeling is constant or changing. It may pulse, spread, contract, sharpen, fade, or remain steady. Report what is happening without demanding movement.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Try a quiet sentence: this feeling is here, and I am also here with support beneath me. Keep only the part that feels true.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Include the hands and feet again. Feel the body's boundaries. Emotion can be present without becoming the whole body or the whole moment.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Perhaps there is an impulse to act, speak, hide, fix, or escape. Notice the impulse as energy. You do not need to obey or suppress it during this breath.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Ask gently: what would make the next minute more workable? More distance, movement, water, contact with someone, quiet, or no immediate action may be the honest answer.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return to the room. Find a color, a straight line, a curved shape, and the farthest sound you can hear. Let orientation widen the field around emotion.

[Pause 40 seconds]

If the feeling still seems manageable, visit one small area once more. Notice it for the length of one inhale and one exhale, then come back to support.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Recognize the choice you have practiced: approach, pause, step back, or stop. Making room does not mean surrendering control or forcing exposure.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Let the emotional label go. Feel the whole body sitting or resting. There may be change, no change, relief, tiredness, or mixed experience. Nothing needs to be declared complete.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Begin to move fingers, toes, shoulders, or the head. Keep the gaze open and orient toward what comes next in the real room.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Choose one practical act of care after the practice, even if it is simply standing slowly or taking a drink. You made room in small, chosen amounts while keeping contact with support.
