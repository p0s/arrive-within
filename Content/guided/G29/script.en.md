# When the Inner Critic Speaks

Find a steady seated or resting position. This practice works with ordinary self-critical thoughts. Choose a mild example, not the most painful judgment you carry, and return to neutral sensation whenever needed.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel the body's contact with support. Notice the room, one sound, and the natural movement of breathing. These are present before any thought is examined.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Recall a recent moment when an inner critical voice appeared. Keep the situation at a distance, like a short headline rather than a detailed replay.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice one sentence the critic used. Perhaps it began with you always, you never, you should, or you are not enough. Hear it as a sentence, not a verdict.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Add a simple frame: I am noticing the thought that. Then repeat only the core idea. See whether this creates even a little room around the words.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel the feet or hands again. A thought can be loud while the body is also touching something solid. Both facts can exist at once.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Ask what the critical thought may be trying to prevent: failure, rejection, embarrassment, uncertainty, or loss of control. Understanding a protective aim does not make the harsh method useful.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice the tone. Would the same information be clearer without contempt, exaggeration, or insult? Separate any practical message from the way it was delivered.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Translate the thought into specific, workable language. Instead of I ruin everything, perhaps: one part did not go as planned, and I can decide what to repair. Use facts you can verify.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Let that revised sentence settle. It does not need to be positive. It needs to be accurate enough to guide the next step without attacking your worth.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If the critic argues back, notice another thought. You do not need to win a debate. Return to contact with support and repeat the more accurate wording once.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Consider whether the situation belongs to your responsibility, someone else's responsibility, shared responsibility, or simple uncertainty. Do not take more ownership than the facts support.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Name one thing you can do, learn, ask, repair, or leave alone. Keep it small enough to be real. If no action is possible now, recognizing that limit can be accurate too.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Try a respectful response: I can take responsibility without reducing myself to this moment. Adjust the words so they fit your values and the actual situation.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice any change in shoulders, jaw, breath, or belly. There may be relief, resistance, no change, or a mixture. The body's response is information, not a score.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Imagine hearing the critical sentence again later today. Rehearse three moves: recognize it as a thought, find the factual message, choose a respectful next sentence.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Let the example fade. Return to sounds, light, and the whole body's position. You are more than the thought, and you do not need that claim to feel inspiring for it to be accurate.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Offer one closing phrase: clear is more useful than cruel. Or choose your own brief reminder. Let it accompany one natural breath.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Move when ready. You practiced listening for useful information without handing a harsh voice authority over your value.
