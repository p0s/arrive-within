# Grief and Tenderness

Choose this practice only where you are safe to pause. Keep your eyes open, change position, or stop at any time. You do not need to bring a painful memory closer. If attention to grief feels too intense, return to the room, the ground, or an ordinary task. Reaching out to someone you trust is also a valid next step.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Begin with what supports you physically. Feel the floor, the chair, the bed, or the wall. Notice the clearest edge of your body: the feet inside socks, a hand resting on fabric, or the back meeting a surface.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Look around slowly, if your eyes are open. Let them rest on something plain and steady. A corner, a color, a line of light. You are here, in this room, at this time.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Grief can appear as sadness, numbness, anger, love, tiredness, relief, confusion, or nothing that has a name. There is no correct feeling to find. Notice only what is already present and manageable enough to meet.

[Pause 18 seconds]

If there is a body sensation connected with this moment, locate its edges rather than entering its center. Perhaps tightness in the throat, weight behind the eyes, hollowness in the chest, or heaviness in the arms. Let the sensation be one part of a larger body that is also touching support.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Move attention between that sensation and a more neutral place. Grief for one breath. Feet for one breath. Grief, if it is workable. The support beneath you. You decide how close to come.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Now notice the breath without using it to push anything away. The inhale may be shallow. The exhale may catch. Breathing does not have to become calm for this practice to be valid.

[Pause 20 seconds]

You might silently say, this matters to me. Or, this is hard to carry. Choose words that are true without making the moment larger. If no words fit, let the hand rest somewhere that feels steady and say nothing.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Grief often holds a bond: to a person, a place, a possibility, a time, or a part of life that changed. You do not need to explain that bond. For one moment, acknowledge that caring and loss can stand near each other.

[Pause 25 seconds]

If an image or memory arrives, notice that it has arrived. You may let it pass. You may return to the color in the room, the pressure under your body, or the sound farthest away. Protecting your limits is part of this practice.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Offer yourself one modest phrase. May I have room to breathe around this. May I move at the pace I can manage. May I accept support when it is available. If these words feel too warm or distant, use something simpler: one moment at a time.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the whole body now, not only the place that hurts. Notice temperature, clothing, contact, sound, and space. The difficult feeling may remain. It is not the only fact in the room.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Consider what would be kind and practical after this practice. Water. Light. A blanket. Fresh air. A message to someone. A familiar routine. Or simply continuing slowly. You do not need to choose yet; notice which possibility feels least demanding.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice whether any part of you is asking to be understood, and whether another part simply needs quiet. Both can be present. You do not have to make a story from the feeling or find a lesson inside it. For now, the task is smaller: remain in contact with one support, one breath, and the room that is holding this moment with you.

[Pause 22 seconds]

Begin to orient outward. Let your eyes move and your head turn if that is comfortable. Feel the length of the arms and legs. Press gently into the surface beneath you and release.

[Pause 15 seconds]

There is no finish line for grief, and this practice is not a treatment. It is one careful interval in which you did not have to carry the whole experience at once.

Take the time you need before standing or moving on.
