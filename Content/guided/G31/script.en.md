# Patience

Find a seated or resting position that is workable, not perfect. Patience here does not mean tolerating harm or delaying a needed action. It means staying for one chosen, ordinary moment without demanding that it move faster.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel the body arriving on its support. Notice one place that already feels settled and one place that is still adjusting.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Choose a neutral sensation to accompany: the hands touching, the feet on the floor, or breath moving somewhere in the body.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Stay until the sensation changes once. Perhaps pressure shifts, temperature becomes clearer, or an inhale turns toward an exhale. There is no need to make the change happen.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice any urge to move on, check the time, improve the practice, or reach the next instruction. Let the urge be present for one breath before deciding anything.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Patience can be small. Try the phrase: I can stay for this one breath. Let the sentence end when the breath ends.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Begin again with the next breath. Not forever. Not until everything is resolved. Only this ordinary beginning, middle, and end.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If discomfort needs an adjustment, make it slowly. Patience includes responding carefully rather than refusing what the body is saying.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Listen to a sound until it ends or fades from attention. Notice the space before another sound becomes clear. You are not waiting for a special event.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Perhaps thoughts are forecasting how long something in life will take. Acknowledge planning, then return to the immediate contact under the body.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the difference between waiting with tension and waiting with room. Soften one unnecessary grip in the jaw, hands, shoulders, or belly without requiring full relaxation.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Choose three breaths. For each one, notice the first moment, allow the middle, and recognize the ending. If attention leaves, return to whichever breath is here now.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Let counting go. Notice whether impatience has vanished, changed, or stayed. None of these outcomes determines whether you practiced well.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring to mind one situation today where a pause could create a little more choice. Keep the image brief. Do not solve the whole situation here.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Imagine feeling both feet before responding, or taking one breath before the next action. Patience can be a gap large enough for a wise decision.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return to the room. Notice sounds, light, and the full outline of the body. Let the next moment arrive without leaning toward it.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Move when you choose. You practiced staying without becoming stuck and waiting without abandoning action.
