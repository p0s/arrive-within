# Gratitude Without Force

Settle in a supported position. This practice does not ask you to feel grateful, deny difficulty, or find a bright side. It simply makes room to notice something that is quietly supportive, if anything is available.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Begin with direct contact. Feel a surface holding some of the body's weight. It does not need to feel comforting to be doing useful work.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice one ordinary condition that helps this moment happen: air to breathe, a place to sit, clothing, light, or enough safety to pause briefly.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Choose only what feels true. If none of these fits, stay with the neutral fact of contact beneath the body.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let the chosen support be specific. Not life is good, but this chair is holding me. Not everything is fine, but this glass of water is available.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice the body's response. There may be warmth, softness, irritation, grief, numbness, or no change. Gratitude is not measured by a pleasant sensation.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Consider one effort made by another person that reached you, directly or indirectly. It might be recent or ordinary: preparing food, maintaining a path, writing instructions, offering time.

[Pause 30 seconds]

You do not need to idealize the person or erase complexity. Recognize only the particular helpful act.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Try the words: this helped. Let that be enough. If the words do not fit, release them.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice one capacity in yourself that supported the day: asking for help, pausing, adapting, continuing, resting, or noticing a limit.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Recognition does not need to become pride. Simply include your own effort in the field of what has carried this moment.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Now hold difficulty and support side by side. Something may be genuinely hard, and something small may still be helping. Neither fact cancels the other.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Return to the most concrete support you noticed. Feel or picture it once more. Allow appreciation if it comes; allow neutrality if it does not.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Widen attention to sounds, light, and the whole body. Let the inventory end without trying to preserve a mood.

[Pause 20 seconds]

As you move on, consider one simple response: use the support, care for it, thank someone, or just remember it. You practiced noticing without requiring positivity.
