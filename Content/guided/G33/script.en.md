# Morning Arrival

Meet the morning in a position that is safe and supported. You do not have to feel rested, positive, or ready. Begin with the morning that is actually here.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Notice the light. It may be daylight, a lamp, or darkness that has not yet lifted. Let the eyes receive brightness and shadow without needing to judge the hour.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Feel where the body is supported. Notice the weight of the feet, seat, back, or hands. If the body feels heavy, restless, or uncomfortable, that is information rather than a problem to solve in this moment.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Let one natural breath move through. Notice where it is easiest to feel: nose, throat, chest, belly, or the movement of clothing. Leave the breath at its own depth and pace.

[Pause 18 seconds]

The day may already be speaking in lists. Hear those plans without stepping into all of them. For now, let each item wait outside this single breath.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Notice the inner weather. Clear or foggy, eager or reluctant, calm or busy. More than one quality can be present. No forecast is needed for the whole day.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Choose one way you would like to meet the next hour. Perhaps steadily, kindly, directly, patiently, or with enough space to notice. Pick a word that feels workable rather than impressive.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Feel how that word might appear in the body. It could mean easing the jaw, placing both feet down, looking at one thing at a time, or taking a pause before replying. Let the word become one small physical choice.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Now name the first practical step after this meditation. Keep it close: drink water, open a curtain, wash, dress, eat, or begin one task. There is no need to organize the entire day here.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Take in the room again. Notice light, sound, and support together. This morning does not need to resemble any other morning to count as a beginning.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Move the hands and feet. Let the gaze settle toward your first step. Begin the day from where you are, at a pace you can inhabit.
