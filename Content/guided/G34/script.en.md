# Awake and Grounded

Choose a seated posture that feels stable. If you are very sleepy, unwell, or responsible for driving or another safety-critical task, rest or address that need instead of relying on meditation to create alertness.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Let the spine find a little length without becoming rigid. Feel both feet or the full base of support. Stability begins below while attention becomes clearer above.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Open the eyes enough to receive light. Let the gaze rest forward and slightly down. Notice color, edges, and depth without staring at one object.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Feel one ordinary inhale. Do not make it deeper. Notice where the body expands or lifts, then let the exhale return without collapsing the posture.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Bring attention to sounds. Hear the nearest sound, then the farthest one available. Let listening widen the sense of the room.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Notice temperature on the face and hands. Coolness, warmth, moving air, or no clear difference can all serve as fresh information.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Press the feet lightly into the floor or the body into its support, then release the extra effort. Feel the brief increase in contact and the return to ordinary pressure.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Roll the shoulders slowly once if movement is comfortable. Let the head balance over the spine. Alertness can include movement rather than fighting it.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Choose three breaths to meet at their beginning. Each time an inhale first becomes noticeable, recognize: a new breath is here.

[Pause 40 seconds]

If thought becomes foggy, sharpen one channel at a time. See one edge clearly. Hear one sound clearly. Feel one point of contact clearly.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Now include all three: seeing, hearing, and touch. Let the field be bright enough to stay interested and broad enough to remain settled.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice whether alertness has become tension in the jaw, brow, or hands. Soften unnecessary effort while keeping the eyes and posture awake.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring to mind the first useful task of the day. Name it in a few concrete words. Avoid loading it with the whole day's expectations.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Imagine beginning only the first visible step. Feel the feet and one breath as you picture that beginning.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Return to immediate sensation. Light, sound, contact, breath. Let these signals organize attention without demanding enthusiasm.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Take one slightly fuller stretch only if it feels natural: lengthen the arms, lift the chest, or move the neck gently. Then settle into a steady upright shape.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Look around the room deliberately. Name where you are and what comes next. Orientation turns wakefulness toward a real situation.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Move into the next part of the day at a workable pace. You practiced being awake without becoming hurried and grounded without becoming dull.
