# Intention for the Day

Settle into a supported seated position. An intention is not a prediction, promise, or demand that the day unfold a certain way. It is a modest quality you can return to while responding to what actually happens.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel the body here before considering the day. Notice feet or support, one breath, and the light in the room.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let the day appear in broad outline. There may be work, care, travel, rest, uncertainty, or tasks you cannot yet see. Avoid rehearsing every detail.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Ask: how would I like to meet what comes? Listen for a simple quality rather than an outcome. Perhaps steadiness, clarity, patience, courage, openness, care, or appropriate boundaries.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Choose one word that feels relevant and believable. If no word arrives, use: present for the next step.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice how the word lands in the body. It may feel meaningful, neutral, awkward, or distant. You do not need a strong feeling for the intention to be usable.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Connect the intention to one concrete moment. If the quality is patience, perhaps feel both feet before answering a difficult message. If it is clarity, perhaps name the next single task.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Picture only that small action. Keep the image ordinary and within your influence. Let the rest of the day remain unknown.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Return to one breath. On the inhale, remember the chosen word. On the exhale, release any need to perform it perfectly.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Consider what might make the intention difficult: fatigue, pressure, interruption, fear, or habit. Acknowledge one likely obstacle without treating it as failure in advance.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Choose a cue that can help you remember. A doorway, unlocking the phone, sitting down, or taking a drink can become a brief invitation to return.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Imagine encountering that cue. Feel one contact point, take one natural breath, and recall the word. The return can take only a few seconds.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Notice whether the intention needs a boundary. Care may include saying no. Courage may include asking for help. Patience may include stopping a harmful delay.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Refine the wording so it does not erase your needs or anyone else's reality. A useful intention guides behavior without demanding control over outcomes.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Try a complete sentence: today, when I remember, I will meet the next workable moment with clarity. Replace clarity with your own word.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Let the sentence go. Rest for several breaths without planning. Feel the body's weight and let sounds arrive.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Bring the word back once more. Notice that forgetting later will not invalidate it. Each remembering is a fresh opportunity, not evidence about your character.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Open awareness to the room. Identify the actual first step after this practice: stand, drink water, open a document, rest, or speak with someone.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Begin moving when ready. Carry one modest direction rather than a demand for the whole day. Let reality keep informing how the intention is lived.
