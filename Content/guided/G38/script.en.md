# Preparing for Sleep

Settle in bed or another safe resting place. This practice can support the transition toward sleep, but it cannot promise sleep or replace care for persistent sleep difficulty. You do not need to finish the recording. If you are awake at the end, nothing has gone wrong.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Adjust temperature, light, clothing, blankets, and position as much as you reasonably can. Let practical comfort come before meditation.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Notice the places where the body meets the bed or support. Feel the broad contact of head, back, pelvis, legs, heels, arms, or whichever areas are resting.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Let the surface carry its share of the weight. You do not need to become heavy. Notice where weight is already being received.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel one ordinary breath. There is no special sleep breath to achieve. Let the inhale arrive in its own size and the exhale leave in its own time.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Bring attention to the forehead. Notice warmth, coolness, pressure, movement, or little sensation. Let the muscles around the eyes do only the work they still need to do.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel the jaw and mouth. If the teeth are touching with effort, allow a little space. Let the tongue find an easy place without arranging it precisely.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Move awareness to the neck and shoulders. Imagine no change. Simply feel how these areas are positioned and where support reaches them.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Notice both arms from shoulders to fingertips. Feel their length, temperature, and weight. One side may be clearer than the other.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Let attention rest in the hands. Fingers may curl, spread, touch fabric, or remain difficult to feel. Nothing needs correcting.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Bring awareness to the chest and upper back. Breathing moves through this region while the bed continues to hold the body. Let movement and support be known together.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice the belly. It may rise, widen, settle, tighten, or remain subtle. Do not push it toward softness. Follow two ordinary breaths as they are.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Feel the lower back and pelvis. This central weight may be broad, uneven, comfortable, restless, or numb. If an adjustment is needed, make it slowly and begin again.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Move down through both thighs. Notice where they touch the bed and where there is space. Let the legs be supported without asking them to feel relaxed.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Include the knees, lower legs, ankles, and feet. Sense the whole path from pelvis to toes. Temperature and pressure may change from one area to the next.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Now feel the body as one resting shape. Some areas may be vivid and others almost absent. Let the map be incomplete.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Sounds may continue inside or outside the room. Rather than waiting for perfect silence, let each sound appear and pass within a larger quiet.

[Pause 45 seconds]

If thoughts about tomorrow arrive, notice planning. If thoughts about today arrive, notice remembering. You do not need to finish either activity now.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Use a brief phrase if helpful: this can wait until waking. Repeat it once, then return to weight against the bed.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice the darkness or dim light through the eyelids. Shapes, colors, movement, or plain darkness can drift without your attention following them.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Let the breath become less interesting. You do not need to monitor every cycle. Feel only an occasional exhale, then return to the whole resting body.

[Pause 55 seconds]

Perhaps sleepiness is present as heaviness, blurred thought, warmth, drifting images, or small gaps in attention. There is no need to check whether it is working.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Perhaps wakefulness remains clear. Let wakefulness be a condition you are resting with, not an opponent you must defeat.

[Pause 50 seconds]

For several moments, release the practice itself. No body scan, no counting, no task. Let the bed hold the body while sounds and breath continue without supervision.

[Pause 70 seconds]

If attention gathers again, feel one broad area of contact: the back, side, legs, or head. Let that single fact be enough.

[Pause 55 seconds]

You may stop listening now. There is nothing further you need to achieve. Sleep may come, or rest may continue as rest.

[Pause 60 seconds]

If you remain awake and choose to move, do so gently and follow whatever sleep-care plan is appropriate for you. For now, let this moment be quiet, unfinished, and free from a test.
