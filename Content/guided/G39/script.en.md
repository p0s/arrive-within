# Body into Bed

Settle in a safe resting position. Adjust pillows, blankets, and posture before beginning. This practice is about letting the bed carry more weight, not forcing the body to become relaxed or still.

[Pause 12 seconds]

Feel the place where the head meets support. Notice the size and shape of that contact. Let the surface receive what it already receives.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Bring attention to the shoulders. One may rest more fully than the other. Allow the difference without trying to make both sides match.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel the upper back spreading across the bed. On an exhale, notice whether even a small amount of weight is handed over. If not, stay with the simple contact.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Move to the arms. Sense upper arms, elbows, forearms, and hands. Let each region have its own weight and temperature.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Notice the chest moving with breath and the back remaining supported beneath that movement. The body can move and be held at the same time.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel the belly and lower ribs. Do not push them down. Let the bed receive the back of the body while the front remains free to breathe.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Bring awareness to the lower back and pelvis. This may be one of the heaviest regions. Notice where pressure is broad, concentrated, comfortable, or unclear.

[Pause 40 seconds]

If the position asks for a small adjustment, make it. Then feel the new pattern of contact rather than judging whether it is better.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Move into the thighs. Let the large muscles stop performing for a few moments. They do not need to feel soft; they only need permission not to act.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Feel the knees and lower legs. Notice fabric, air, warmth, coolness, and the places where the bed rises to meet them.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Bring attention to heels and feet. Let their weight travel down into support. Toes can curl, spread, or remain as they are.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Now sense the whole line of the body from head to feet. Imagine no sinking. Feel only the actual distribution of weight already present.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice one region that still seems to hold itself away from the bed. Offer it the possibility of being supported, without demanding a response.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Let three ordinary exhales pass. With each one, recognize a different contact area: head, torso, then legs.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Release the scan. Let attention become broad and less precise. The bed does not need your supervision to keep carrying the body.

[Pause 45 seconds]

If sleepiness comes, you can stop listening. If wakefulness remains, continue resting with the simple fact of support.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Nothing had to melt or disappear. You practiced allowing one region at a time to be met by the bed.
