# Quieting the Mind

Settle in bed or another safe resting place. A quiet mind does not have to be empty. This practice changes how you relate to bedtime thinking without promising sleep or requiring thoughts to stop.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Feel the body supported. Notice one broad contact area and one sound in the room. Let the present environment be available alongside thought.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Observe the next thought that appears. Do not search for one and do not finish it deliberately. Notice that a thought can arrive by itself.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Give it a light label: planning, remembering, rehearsing, worrying, imagining, or simply thinking. Let the label describe the activity rather than the truth of the content.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Return to the weight of the body for one breath. Thought is present, and support is present. Neither needs to remove the other.

[Pause 35 seconds]

When another thought appears, imagine placing its subject on a page for tomorrow. You do not need to picture the page clearly or trust that every detail will be remembered.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Use the phrase: noted for later. Then let attention come back to the bed beneath you.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Some thoughts may repeat. Recognize repetition as the mind checking the same door again. You can answer with the same brief phrase rather than a new argument.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Notice the gap after one thought fades and before another becomes clear. The gap may be a second, a fraction of a second, or not easy to find. Do not hold it open.

[Pause 35 seconds]

Let sounds be more interesting than thoughts for a while. Hear near and far sounds without naming their source. Let each one end on its own.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Return to the body. Feel the forehead, jaw, and hands. If these areas are working around a thought, let them reduce effort by one small degree.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If a practical concern truly needs action tonight, acknowledge that choice. You may pause the practice and act. Quieting the mind does not mean ignoring safety or responsibility.

[Pause 35 seconds]

If no action is needed now, try: nothing to solve in this breath. Feel one inhale and one exhale without using them to force calm.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Imagine thoughts as messages passing across a dim screen. You can notice that a message exists without opening every thread.

[Pause 40 seconds]

If imagery is unhelpful, return to simple language: thought, then contact. Thought, then sound. Move between them without judgment.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice whether the mind is asking, am I asleep yet, is this working, how long has it been. Label checking. Let the question remain unanswered.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Feel the whole resting body. Let awareness be broad enough that no single thought occupies the entire field.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Now release labels for several moments. Thoughts may come with words, images, fragments, or unfinished shapes. Allow them to be incomplete.

[Pause 60 seconds]

If you find yourself following a story, return at the moment you notice. There is no penalty for having followed it and no need to retrace the path.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Let the next three thoughts pass without response if you can. If one captures attention, begin again with the next one.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Notice that the mind may be active while the body still rests. Rest does not have to wait for complete mental silence.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Let attention settle on the darkest or quietest part of present experience: closed eyes, distant sound, the bed's steady contact, or the pause after an exhale.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Stay lightly. If sleepiness comes, stop following the instructions. If wakefulness remains, let this be a period of resting rather than a test of sleep.

[Pause 55 seconds]

There is nothing to empty and nothing to finish here. Let thought become one small kind of movement inside a larger, supported night.
