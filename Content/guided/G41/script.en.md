# Awake at Night

You are awake. That is the simple truth of this moment. You do not have to decide yet what it means for tomorrow.

Let the screen rest face down or dim. Settle in a position that is as comfortable as it can reasonably be. This practice does not promise sleep. Its purpose is to make a little less struggle around being awake.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Feel where the bed receives your weight. Notice the back of the head, the shoulders, the back, the hips, the legs, or whatever points are clear. You do not need to scan them all. Choose one place that feels neutral enough.

[Pause 15 seconds]

Let the breath move without making it deeper. At night, effort can become another source of wakefulness. Allow breathing to be small, uneven, or ordinary.

[Pause 18 seconds]

If the mind is calculating hours, planning tomorrow, or asking why you are still awake, notice that this is what minds do when certainty is unavailable. You do not need to answer every question in the dark.

Silently offer the words, not now. Not as a command, but as permission to postpone the problem until daylight.

[Pause 18 seconds]

Listen for the quietest sound you can hear. Then notice the space around that sound. Let hearing be broad and easy.

[Pause 20 seconds]

Return to the bed holding you. Imagine no sinking and no special heaviness. Simply notice that you are not required to hold all of your weight by yourself.

[Pause 20 seconds]

If restlessness is strong, a small change of position is allowed. Move slowly enough to feel the movement, then let the body settle again. There is no perfect position you must find.

[Pause 15 seconds]

For the next few breaths, count only the exhale. One. Then begin again at one. There is nowhere to reach and no need to remember the previous number.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Thoughts can pass through like distant rooms with lights on. You do not have to enter each room. Feel one point of contact. Hear one sound. Receive one breath.

[Pause 30 seconds]

If you notice yourself checking whether the practice is working, include that too. Checking is another thought, not a verdict. Return to something that does not need an answer: the temperature around your hands, the weight of the blanket, or the quiet movement of an exhale.

[Pause 25 seconds]

Let time be unmeasured for a while. You do not need to know how many minutes have passed. The night can be made of this one breath, and then another. Each moment of resting is still rest, even when sleep has not arrived.

[Pause 30 seconds]

You may fall asleep. You may remain awake. Either way, this moment can contain less argument.

Let the next pause be longer. The practice can continue without your help.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Nothing to finish. Nothing to prove. Just the body here, held for another ordinary night.
