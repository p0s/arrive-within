# Back to Sleep

You are awake, and you are here. Settle in a safe resting position. This practice offers a quiet path through wakefulness; it cannot promise sleep or replace professional care for ongoing sleep difficulty. You may stop listening at any time.

[Pause 15 seconds]

If something practical needs attention—pain, temperature, medication, a child, an alarm, or safety—take care of it first. Meditation does not ask you to ignore a real need.

[Pause 25 seconds]

When you are ready, let the clock remain unchecked if that is workable. You do not need a number to know that you are awake now.

[Pause 30 seconds]

Feel one broad place where the body meets the bed: the back, one side, the head, or the legs. Let that contact be more important than measuring the night.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Notice the room as it is. Darkness or dim light. Near or distant sound. Warm or cool air. Let orientation replace the sense of being lost in time.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Feel an ordinary breath. Do not deepen or slow it. Follow only the exhale, then let attention return to the body's weight.

[Pause 40 seconds]

If the mind says you must sleep now, recognize urgency. The thought is understandable, and you do not have to carry out its demand in this breath.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Try a quieter sentence: for now, I can rest while awake. You do not need to like wakefulness or choose it for the sentence to be true.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Bring attention to the forehead and eyes. Notice whether they are searching, focusing, or trying. Let them receive darkness without looking for sleep.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Feel the jaw, tongue, and throat. If effort can reduce slightly, allow it. If it remains, simply know where it is.

[Pause 40 seconds]

Move to shoulders and arms. Let the bed or blankets meet their weight. Hands can rest without holding the night together.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Feel the chest and upper back. Breathing may be quiet, uneven, shallow, or full. Let it move without becoming a project.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Notice the belly and lower back. There may be worry, tension, warmth, or little sensation. Keep the area within the larger support of the bed.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Feel the pelvis, legs, and feet. Let attention travel slowly downward, then rest at the heels or toes for several moments.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Sense the whole body as one shape lying in a room at night. You do not need every region to feel clear or relaxed.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Thoughts may be reviewing tomorrow. Label planning. Thoughts may be replaying today. Label remembering. Thoughts may calculate consequences. Label forecasting.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Each time, answer with the same small movement: thought, then bed. Thought, then sound. Let repetition make the response simpler rather than more forceful.

[Pause 55 seconds]

If a useful idea appears, trust that you can address it after waking, or use the note-taking approach that is already part of your sleep routine. Do not build a new plan in bed now.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Listen for the farthest sound you can hear. Then the nearest. Then let all sounds share one field without ranking them.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Notice the quiet after a sound ends. Quiet does not have to be complete. It can be the space in which another sound appears.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Return to contact beneath the body. Find the heaviest area. Let the surface receive its weight without imagining that it must sink.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Find the lightest or least clear area. Include its uncertainty. A body scan can have blank places.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Now release the scan. Let awareness become broad, soft, and imprecise. The body continues to breathe and the bed continues to support it without detailed attention.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Perhaps images or fragments begin to drift. Do not check whether they mean sleep is close. Let them form and dissolve without following.

[Pause 55 seconds]

Perhaps the mind remains sharp. Let sharpness be present inside a body that is resting. Wakefulness is not an action you must keep performing.

[Pause 55 seconds]

Try counting only exhales from one to five, lightly. One. Then the next exhale, two. If you lose the count, begin at one without evaluating.

[Pause 75 seconds]

Let counting go after five or whenever it becomes work. Rest with the uncounted rhythm.

[Pause 60 seconds]

If frustration appears, locate one physical trace of it. Pressure, heat, tightness, movement. Notice a small area, then return to a larger neutral contact point.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Say quietly: frustration is here; the bed is also here. Keep both facts in the same moment.

[Pause 50 seconds]

You may choose to change position. Move slowly, settle blankets again, and feel the new contact map. A deliberate adjustment is not starting over.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Let the next minute contain no method. No counting, labeling, scanning, or trying. Instructions will pause while the night continues.

[Pause 70 seconds]

If you are still listening, notice one exhale. Then feel both hands or both feet, wherever they rest.

[Pause 45 seconds]

Bring to mind a plain, non-demanding place: a familiar room, a quiet path, a simple patch of sky. Do not make a vivid story. Let the image remain incomplete and still.

[Pause 55 seconds]

Notice one neutral detail of that place, then let it fade. Return to darkness, sound, and weight.

[Pause 50 seconds]

Sleep may arrive without a clear boundary. If attention becomes scattered or the words stop making sense, allow that. You do not need to come back to the instruction.

[Pause 60 seconds]

If sleep does not arrive, the practice remains a period of reduced struggle. Rest has value without being used as a guarantee.

[Pause 55 seconds]

Feel the whole resting shape once more. Let the forehead be as it is, the breath be as it is, and the night be unfinished.

[Pause 60 seconds]

Release the wish to know what happens next. For this moment, nothing needs to be decided.

[Pause 60 seconds]

You can stop listening now. Let the remaining silence belong to sleep if sleep comes, and to quiet wakefulness if it does not.

[Pause 70 seconds]

If you later choose to leave the bed or follow another step in your established sleep-care plan, do so gently and safely. Until then, let the bed carry the body through this one unhurried moment.
